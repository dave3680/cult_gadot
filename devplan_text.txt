CULT OF ACCUMULATION
Development Plan & Rebalance Roadmap
Version 1.0 — March 2026
Executive Summary
Cult of Accumulation has strong bones. The doctrine system is distinctive, the breeding loop creates genuine attachment to individual followers, and the combo trait web gives depth that rewards experimentation. The problem is not concept — it's execution and balance.
Three issues are killing the fun right now:
The scoring formula scales so poorly across 10 weeks that the game effectively has two modes: trivially easy (weeks 1-4) and mathematically impossible (weeks 7-10) unless the player landed a specific multiplier chain.
The relic pool is thin and incoherent. Most relics are additive bumps that become irrelevant by week 5. There is no moment where a relic transforms how the game feels.
The breeding system is the game's most interesting mechanic, but it is too slow, too passive, and too disconnected from scoring to feel like the engine it should be.
This document is a prioritised plan to fix all three, add a relic pool of 100 new cards, and turn Cult of Accumulation into something that earns one-more-run addiction.

Part 2: Core Rebalance
The central problem is that 1.5x exponential growth per week creates a gap that additive relics cannot bridge. The entire late game depends on finding a multiplier chain. Everything below is designed to make more builds viable across all 10 weeks.
2.1 Target Curve — Recalibrate for Two Viable Paths
The current curve hits 1540 by week 10. That is not the problem. The problem is the jump between week 4 (135) and week 7 (455) — a 3.4x increase where the player has had at most 3 shops to prepare. Smooth this out.

2.2 Doctrine Scaling — Make Each Doctrine Matter at Week 8
FLESH and RUIN use fixed additive values that become noise by mid-game. Replace them with percentage-based scaling tied to the weekly target.
Doctrine
New Scaling Formula
FLESH (3+ BLOOD)
Bonus = floor(weekly_target × 0.08). At week 6 target 200, this gives +16. At week 10 target 1000, it gives +80. Replaces flat +6.
FLESH (0 BLOOD)
Penalty = floor(weekly_target × 0.04). Keeps the sting proportional.
RUIN (top BONE condition)
Bonus = floor(weekly_target × 0.10) + (highest_bone_tier × 2). Additive scaled to target, with tier multiplied for meaningful late-game reward.
SILENCE (exactly 1 VOID)
Keep exponent +1. Silence scales naturally because the multiplier system is multiplicative. No change needed.
Why not multiply instead of scale additively?
FLESH and RUIN are additive doctrines. Making them multiplicative would require restructuring the hook order and likely break the scoring flow. Percentage-of-target keeps them additive while preserving relevance throughout the run.
2.3 VOID Follower Tier — Open the Refined VOID Path
Currently, VOID followers are always tier 0, making refined_void_count (the ×1.5 bonus per refined VOID) nearly unreachable. The entire SILENCE doctrine endgame is locked behind a single Soul Lantern use. Fix this by opening two new routes to refined VOID:
Wild breeding between two VOID followers (currently 30% chance) should produce offspring at tier 1 instead of tier 0. A VOID-VOID pair producing another tier-0 VOID is pointless.
Nest breeding with two VOID parents should produce a tier 1-2 VOID baby (using the standard ±1 variance around a floor(0+0/2)=0 base, but with a minimum floor of 1).
Add 2-3 relics in the new pool that allow VOID followers to gain tier from scoring (e.g. 'each time a VOID follower is sacrificed, it gains +1 tier, max 5'). This creates a VOID investment arc.
The Soul Lantern can remain the fastest route to high-tier VOID, but it should no longer be the only route.
2.4 Blood Economy — Cap Overflow, Raise Floor
Two problems: overflow blood has no cap (a huge combo can generate a bank-breaking windfall in one play), and early game blood is so tight that buying any relic in week 1-2 feels punishing.
Change
Detail
Set overflow_weekly_cap
Cap overflow blood at floor(weekly_target × 0.5). Week 1 cap: 20. Week 10 cap: 500. Prevents snowball while still rewarding clean plays.
Starting blood
Give the player 5 blood at run start. Week 1 shop becomes accessible for all doctrines without requiring a lucky early win.
Early win bonus
Keep the +20 blood early-win bonus. It's meaningful and good.
Recruit cost
Keep at 1 blood each. Recruits are already appropriately priced.
2.5 The Round Cap — Change to Per-Week
Currently the 1.25x round cap applies per individual round, not per week. As documented in the open questions: a player could theoretically score 2.5x the weekly target across two rounds. This is not exploitable in practice today, but it will be once relics are stronger. Change the cap to track total weekly devotion across both rounds and cap at floor(target × 2.0). This gives players who narrowly fail round 1 a real chance in round 2 without allowing unlimited catch-up.
Part 3: New Systems
3.1 Legendary Guarantee — Implement _offer_legendary_if_needed()
This stub exists for a reason. Implement it: if the player reaches week 6 without having seen a single legendary relic offered (not necessarily bought), guarantee one legendary in the shop that week. This is standard roguelite design — it prevents runs that feel impossible through bad luck alone.
Implementation: track legendary_seen_in_shop: bool in GameState. Set it true whenever a legendary appears in any shop slot. Call _offer_legendary_if_needed() on shop generation for weeks 6+. If !legendary_seen_in_shop, force one slot to roll legendary.
3.2 Meta-Progression — The Codex
Currently there is no meta-progression. Each run starts completely fresh. This is true to the roguelite genre, but it means there is no reason to play a second run after winning once. Balatro keeps players coming back through unlocks and joker discovery. Cult of Accumulation needs the same hook.
Proposed: The Codex. A persistent (across-runs) record of followers, relics, and combo traits the player has discovered. Each new discovery adds a flavour entry. No gameplay bonuses — discovery only. This is enough to motivate completionists and give returning players a sense of progress. The Codex also reveals relic descriptions for items the player has purchased at least once, removing the information asymmetry that currently punishes new players.
3.3 Nest Expansion — Unlock a Third Nest
Two nests is fine for weeks 1-5. By week 7, a breeding-focused player has a pool full of interesting followers and nowhere to put them. Add a third nest slot, either as a purchasable shop item (cost: 25 blood) or as a week 5 milestone reward. This makes breeding a meaningful late-game investment axis rather than something that plateaus early.
3.4 Ritual Cards Without Scarlet Planetarium
Ritual cards are gated behind the Scarlet Planetarium relic, which is a RARE shop item that may never appear. A player can finish a run without ever knowing ritual cards exist. Consider offering one ritual card as a free choice at week 3, teaching the mechanic before gating the ongoing system behind a relic. The Planetarium relic then becomes 'access this system permanently' rather than 'access this system at all.'
Part 4: 100 New Relic Cards
These relics are designed to: (a) create new build archetypes not supported by the current 50, (b) scale meaningfully into late game, and (c) generate the 'wait, I can do what?' moments that make Balatro addictive. They are grouped by archetype.
Design principle
Every relic here should change how the player thinks about follower selection — not just add +N to a number. The best relics in Balatro reframe what counts as a good hand. Aim for the same.

Part 5: Development Roadmap
Tasks ordered by impact-to-effort ratio. Do not start Phase 2 until Phase 1 is complete and tested.
Phase 1 — Stabilise (1-2 weeks)
Get the game working correctly before changing anything. These are fixes, not features.
Set RUN_ONLY_SIM = false in TestRunner.gd. Run the full test suite. Fix every failure before touching balance numbers.
Delete dead code: draw_new_hand(), get_shop_rarity_slots(), _deferred_change_scene stub.
Fix Soul Lantern description to document the blood drain. Decide: is this intentional? If yes, show the cost clearly. If no, remove it.
Fix Hollow Chant description to match actual mechanic ('each copy adds +1 to multiplier exponent').
Fix chosen_veil type from BREEDING to HYBRID.
Differentiate Bone Idol and Ossuary Standards — make one of them do something different (e.g. Ossuary Standards: instead of per-tier, give +5 additive per BONE count threshold).
Implement _offer_legendary_if_needed() — week 6 guaranteed legendary if none seen.
Phase 2 — Core Rebalance (1-2 weeks)
Change the numbers that govern the run arc.
Apply new target curve (target_base = 40, growth = 1.38, manual overrides for weeks 8-10).
Give player 5 starting blood (add to init_starting_pool or run start).
Set overflow_weekly_cap = floor(weekly_target × 0.5).
Apply doctrine scaling formula (percentage-of-target for FLESH and RUIN).
Change round cap from per-round to per-week tracking (max 2.0× weekly target across both rounds).
VOID tier fix: wild VOID-VOID offspring spawn at tier 1; nest VOID-VOID offspring min tier 1.
Raise MULTIPLIER_TRAIT_CAP to 2, or add UI indicator showing which trait was skipped.
Run simulation (3600 games) and verify pass rates match BALANCE_TARGETS. Adjust if needed.
Phase 3 — Relic Pool Expansion (2-3 weeks)
Implement the 100 new relics from Part 4. Suggested implementation order:
Archetype J (High-Risk) first — these are the most mechanical complexity and need the most testing.
Archetype B (VOID Investment) — fixes the broken VOID path.
Archetype I (Breeding Amplifiers) — makes the core engine-building mechanic feel powerful.
Archetypes A, C, D (Tier / BONE / BLOOD) — expands existing archetypes.
Archetypes E, F, G, H (Triune, Pool, Economy, Count) — fills gaps.
Archetype K (Utility) — polish pass.
Re-run simulation after each archetype addition. Ensure no relic combination breaks week 10 pass rate above 85%.
Phase 4 — The Codex & Meta-Progression (1 week)
Add persistent Codex data structure (JSON file, per-device).
Log discoveries: every new relic seen in shop, every new follower trait encountered, every combo trait bred.
Build simple Codex UI (read-only during run, fully browsable from main menu).
Add unlock descriptions for all 150 relics (50 original + 100 new) revealed on first purchase.
Phase 5 — Polish & Feel (ongoing)

Appendix: Why These Changes Make It Addictive
Balatro is addictive for three reasons: every run feels different, high moments feel earned rather than lucky, and there is always one more thing to discover. Here is how each phase addresses those three requirements:
Every Run Feels Different
Currently, doctrines give the illusion of choice but collapse into similar scoring pipelines by week 5. The new relic archetypes create genuinely divergent build paths: a VOID Investment run with Void Recursion and Annihilation Compact feels nothing like a Sacrifice Count run with The Count Absolute and Mass Offering. The 100 new relics, added to the existing 50, mean a player can run 20+ runs without seeing the same build twice.
High Moments Feel Earned
The current game's high moments depend on luck (did a legendary appear?) more than skill (did I build toward something?). The rebalanced target curve means a player who understands the doctrine system and invests in the right archetype from week 2 will reliably make it to week 8. The challenge comes from weeks 8-10, where the target curve steepens again — but the player is now powerful enough to have a shot. Beating week 10 should feel like a consequence of smart play, not a coin flip on whether Hollow Chant appeared.
Always One More Thing to Discover
The Codex addresses this directly. Players who have won once have a reason to return: discover all 150 relics, breed all 50 combo traits, unlock all trophies. Discovery-as-progression is the oldest and most reliable retention mechanic in games. The High-Risk relics (Archetype J) also create stories — the player who cleared week 10 with The Hungry Altar is going to tell someone about it.
Final note on testing
The balance simulation in TestRunner is your most valuable tool and it is currently disabled. Turning it back on and keeping it green should be a hard rule throughout development — every change to a number in GameState.gd should be validated against the simulation before it ships.
